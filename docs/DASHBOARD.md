# Dashboard

`forge dash` serves a single-page dashboard bound to localhost, one server for every project registered on the machine (`forge dash` in a repo registers it; each project keeps its own session pool, ledgers and policy). The page's address names what it shows: `#/overview` (also the bare `/`) and `#/machine` are the machine's own pages, and `#/p/<project>/<view>` is a project's page, so a reload or a bookmark keeps both and Back and Forward move between projects; the `?project=<name>` link `forge dash` prints lands on that project's Pipeline. It is drawn as a smithy at night: gunmetal plates cut at two corners, one silver-blue gleam where agents are at work, and one warm ember for what waits on you (the Forge theme; Ash is the daylight one).

![The Pipeline board in the Forge theme: jobs across the Queued, Plan, Build and Ship columns, two outlined in ember with YOU chips because they wait on a person, running agents on their cards, two PRs with passing and pending checks, and the Foreman and Daemon running in the status bar](../assets/screenshots/pipeline.jpg)

- **Sidebar**: two sections. **General** holds the machine's pages: **Overview** (the default page: a card per registered project with its active jobs, the jobs waiting on you, its Foreman, its sessions and its latest activity, sorted so the projects with the most waiting come first, then the most recently active, with unavailable and missing ones last; a card's name opens its Pipeline, and its waiting count opens the Pipeline on the Waiting filter. Below the cards, two stacked-bar graphs per UTC day over the last 7, 30 or 90 days (default 30, kept for the whole browser), jobs shipped and estimated cost, each stacked by project with a legend of every project's total and share; then the machine cap as a meter of running job sessions, and today's and this month's spend across projects. The counts refresh every 5 s and the graphs every 15 s) and **Machine** (the dashboard's address, the machine cap and the registered projects, read-only, each with the `forge project` command that changes it). **Project** holds the **project switcher** (each registered project, with how many jobs wait on you; a missing or unavailable one can't be chosen; below a rule, **Add project** opens the Add project dialog, and is there even before any project is registered: type the directory of a repo set up with /forge, an absolute path or one starting with `~`, and optionally a name, then press **Add**, and the dashboard registers it as `forge project add` does and opens its Project page; registering never starts its pool, which waits for your Start) and, once a project is chosen, its views in the order you use them, Project, Pipeline, Issues, Agents, Analytics and Evals. A project's own settings are tabs in the Settings dialog (the gear), listed under a **Project** header once a project is chosen, below General and About under a **Forge** header. Choosing another project keeps the view you are on (Issues in one project opens Issues in the other) and closes the job panel, Settings and the palette; the jobs and sessions of the project you leave keep running. The phone strip shows the same split in one row: the machine's pages, the switcher, then the project's views. Layout, filter and window choices, session log filters and the Thread's Progress toggle are kept per project; the theme, language, sidebar and panel widths and the Rendered/Raw choices are kept for the whole browser. At md and up, drag its right edge to resize it between 184 and 360px, or focus the edge and use the arrow keys (Shift for bigger steps, Home for the rail, End for the widest, Enter to switch between the rail and your last width); a double-click restores the default. Dragging or stepping below the narrowest width collapses it to a 64px icon-only rail: the mark, a command button that opens the palette (Cmd-K or Ctrl-K then opens it at once), the Overview and Machine icons, a rule, the switcher's button (it opens the palette with `project ` typed, which lists the projects), the project's view icons with Pipeline's count in the corner, and the gear. Each rail control shows its name in a tooltip on hover or keyboard focus. The width and the rail are kept in this browser, and the job panel and the status bar follow the sidebar's edge.
- **Status bar**: one slim row fixed at the bottom of every project page (the machine's pages have none). On the left, **Needs you** first (an icon-only bell with the **Notifications** tooltip and a count of the decisions and permission prompts waiting on you), then the **Foreman** and the **Daemon**, a pair in the same accent, each with its glyph and a dashed look while it is off: the Foreman is always shown as the supervisor above the pool (on, with a live dot while the office session or any assistant foreman works and a count of all their permission prompts, or **Foreman off**), and the Daemon shows a `Daemon live/cap` count with a live dot while a session works (its state is in the tooltip); in the middle, **FOCUS** and the focused job's id while a job is focused, which opens that job's panel (the bar shows no version, API, sessions, view or spend); on the right, the model the builder runs on and whether the dashboard is connected to `forge dash`. Each indicator opens a popover with its details and controls: the Foreman's live thread at chat size under **Foreman** (its permission prompts, stacked per session, then a session log with its status, model, effort, Permissions, a Start/Stop toggle, filters and subagents, where a **Foreman** / **Assistant foreman <n>** select above it picks the office session's log or an assistant foreman's, assistant 1 first; while it is off, how to turn it on in `.forge/config.yml`); a Foreman row that opens that popover, then a Start/Stop toggle, one row per session (the inbox session, then each job, which opens that job's log) and the inbox session's live log (the **Inbox session log** toggle) under **Daemon**; the permission prompts, then the decisions, PRs, parks and queued requests under **Needs you**; every role's and review lens's model under **Models** (read-only: change them in Settings › Agents); the API versions and **Retry now** under **Connection**. Escape, the close button or a click outside closes a popover.
- **Command bar** (Cmd-K or Ctrl-K focuses the sidebar's **New job or command** input at md and up, and a second press opens the palette; typing or pasting there opens it with your text; on a phone, the command button in the top strip): type a request in plain words to start a job (its title is derived from the text, or give one), or a command with completion: `next [n]`, `sync`, `retro`, `continue <job> [note]` (or `resume`), `approve|revise|rework|answer|cancel <job> [note]`, `issue <ref>`, and `project <name>`, which opens that project's pages (those with jobs waiting on you are listed first). On the machine's own pages, with no project chosen, the bar only opens a project: it lists the projects, and anything else asks you to choose one first. A new job's card appears at once and its job panel opens on the Thread. A job's panel has **Command for this job**, which opens the bar scoped to that job: a *For <job>* chip, and that job's applicable commands listed first (Backspace in the empty input or the chip's remove button drops the scope; free text still starts a new job). The bar's backdrop is a light scrim, so the page or job panel behind it stays readable.
- **Job panel**: opening a job (a card, a row, a chip, the palette or a popover) slides its panel in from the right over the page, which a backdrop dims along with the sidebar and the status bar; the panel runs the full height of the window. A click on the backdrop, Escape or its close button closes it. Drag the panel's left edge (or focus it and use the arrow keys, Shift for bigger steps, Home and End) to resize it, never over the sidebar; one width is kept in this browser for every job, and a double-click restores the default. On a phone the panel is full width. Its Thread and Log tint each message by its sender: Claude cool, the CLI and the supervisor neutral, Forge warm. The header holds the job session's **Permissions**, **Effort** and Start/Stop on every tab. The Thread puts the job's progress lines between the messages (a new stage, a parked gate, each agent's closing reply, checks and reviews per round, the decision, the PR, merge conflicts, notes; the **Progress** button hides them, and they never count as unread), shows a request longer than 2,000 characters cut, with **Show the whole request** to read all of it, and pins a live line under the conversation: the newest running agent and the last thing it did, else the session's state, with the job's cost so far; clicking it opens the Log. For an active job its Details tab opens with **Environment**: **Focus** or **Unfocus** (the same pointer as `forge focus`: it starts and stops no dev server, starts the job's stopped backing services, and runs the configured `checkout` steps, docs/CONFIG.md "Checkout steps"; a failing step shows its error and keeps the previous focus), **Open in editor** (the worktree in `$FORGE_EDITOR`, default `code`, on the machine running `forge dash`), its component endpoints (one per service or app component, with an **Open** link and a live dot on the dev server's), the **Dev server** row (state `running`, `starting`, `down` or `stopped`, its port and whether it answers, Start, Stop, Restart and Logs; when it crashed, its last lines) and the **Backing services** list (each service's isolation, state, health and address, Logs for the ones Forge runs, and Start, Stop and Restart for the job's services as a group: Stop is `forge backing stop`, which keeps their data). Logs opens one live log under the card, rendered as text, with Follow and close. The card refreshes every 5 s while the tab is visible. Stop signals only a dev server the server can prove it started for this job (otherwise use `forge stop`), and nothing here touches another job's processes. The **Project** page opens with **Running environments**: every job with a live dev server or running containers, plus the shared services, with their CPU and memory.
- **PR chips**: a job's PR chip (on its card, its row, its panel and the Needs you popover) opens the pull request on GitHub; resting the mouse on it, or focusing it from the keyboard outside a card, shows GitHub's details for it in a hover card.
- **Needs you** (the bell in the status bar): every human gate (including **Human check**, a CLI check such as the secret scan that only a human can clear), with its document and one-click actions (for an issue to link, and on the spec approval of a job without one: the candidates Forge found, another ref, a new issue, or none; for an agent's repo proposal: each proposed repo with its reason, and **Approve repos** or **Reject repos**). Jobs parked on unfinished dependencies are not gates: they are listed apart, with **Unblocked, resume**, and are neither marked as waiting on you nor counted in the badge. PRs awaiting review and queued requests are listed there too. An old `#/inbox` link (or `#/p/<project>/inbox`) opens the Pipeline with this popover; old `#/<view>` links open that view of the last project you had open, or the Overview.
- **Project**: the configured `components` drawn as a graph, grouped by repo, with their backing services and their build (solid) and runtime (dashed) dependencies, plus the same as a table. Pick a job under **Highlight a job** to light the components it names and dim all but what depends on them downstream. Read-only.
- **Issues**: your tracker's ready items, with their readiness from triage (Needs info and what is missing, or Ready); start one with a click.
- **Agents**: running agents, orchestrator sessions, and the roster with models and prompt hashes.
- **Analytics**: Forge metrics plus token and cost breakdowns by role and model.
- **Evals**: runs, side-by-side comparisons, and a Run eval button.
- **Settings** (the gear, or ⌘, on a Mac and Ctrl+, elsewhere, even from a text field; with no project chosen it has only General and About): **General** holds this browser's preferences, never stored in `config.yml`: Theme (**Forge**, **Ash**, **Temper** (dark, in the heat colours of tempered steel), **Crucible** (dark, molten gold on char with a carmine ember), **Patina** (dark, weathered copper: verdigris on near-black green), **Obsidian** (dark, volcanic glass with a violet sheen), or **System**: Ash on a light OS, Forge on a dark one, live, and never Temper, Crucible, Patina or Obsidian; each theme's segment shows a swatch of its ground, gleam and ember; a theme saved by an earlier version still loads, the dark ones as Forge and the light ones as Ash), **Grain** (the film grain on the ground and the plates; the dark corners stay), **Screen effect** (the scanlines and soft glow on session logs; the rest of each log's frame stays), plus language. The dashboard also honours your OS's reduced-motion setting: overlays open and close at once, nothing slides or fades, the status bar's and the session logs' lamps stay lit and still, Thread messages appear whole instead of typing out, activity shows its end state with no warming or cooling, the live dot's halo does not pulse, and a running step's ring stays still. The rest of Settings is the editable `.forge/config.yml` keys as forms saved through `forge config set`'s write path, `forge doctor` diagnostics with a copyable report and a redacted support bundle to download for a bug report, and the versions Forge is running. Settings are locked while the daemon runs, because any change to `config.yml` stops it.

In a terminal, a foreground `forge dash` follows its banner line with an **activity log**, one timestamped line per thing that happens: `[12:04:31] session web/_inbox spawned (pid 4242)`, `[12:04:33] http GET /api/nope 404 3ms`, `[12:04:40] job web/12-demo builder started`, each session and job named by its project. Its three kinds are `session` (spawns, ends, busy and idle, holds, waits for a slot, errors, stderr and permission prompts), `http` (requests, without their query string; page loads, assets, each project's state poll and event streams only when they fail) and `job` (events appended to a job's `events.jsonl`, never the history). In colour, each session and job has its own colour on every line it appears in, and statuses and states are green, yellow or red ([Output mode](CLI.md#output-mode)). `--quiet` turns it off, and agent mode (`--json`, `FORGE_MODE=agent` or a stdout that is not a terminal) prints only the banner ([Output mode](CLI.md#output-mode)).

The dashboard is a Svelte 5 + TypeScript single-page app. Its built output (`dashboard/dist/`) is committed only on `main`: CI builds it for each release, and the release PR adds it to `main`, so installing the plugin builds and installs nothing. On `develop`, the working branch, `dist/` is gitignored: build it with `npm run build:dashboard`.

Each running agent shows on its job's card, in the drawer header and on the Agents page as one live line: its role, model, how long it has run and the last thing its Claude Code transcript shows (a tool call or a line of text). On the card it sits beside the owning session's liveness (its lease chip); in the drawer header that lease chip sits among the job's badges instead. The drawer's **Agents** tab lists the round's agents with their status, duration and tokens, and follows the chosen one's transcript live; a job driven from an interactive session (not the daemon) shows that session's transcript, read-only, in its Log tab. Transcripts are read from `$CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR/projects`, else `~/.claude/projects`, and only for this repo's own sessions.

Buttons don't run agents directly. They queue requests, and an orchestrator session drains them: your own (`/forge requests`), so permissions and spend stay with you, or, with `daemon.enabled: true`, a headless Claude Code session that `forge dash` supervises and nudges whenever new requests arrive. With the daemon, each job gets its own headless session (up to `daemon.maxConcurrent` at once), and a small inbox session takes requests that have no job yet, so one job's crash never stalls another and each job has a log of its own. The daemon spends tokens unattended, limited to the tools in `daemon.allowedTools`. When an agent needs anything else, its session pauses and the call shows in the status bar's Needs-you popover and in the job drawer's dock with Allow once, Allow for this session and Deny, so you grant it from the dashboard instead of editing config. Each session's log also has a **Permissions** button (its popover offers Ask, Auto, Refuse) that switches that one session's Claude Code permission mode, live, until `forge dash` restarts. The Daemon popover's **Job sessions' permissions** sets the default for every job without its own choice, the running ones included and every job created after it (`forge dash --permission-mode auto` starts with it set); a job's own choice in its panel header overrides it; see [Daemon](CONFIG.md#daemon). A job's log also has an **Effort** button (its popover offers Inherit, Low to Max) that sets the job's reasoning effort, for its agents at once and its session from its next start, whether or not the daemon runs; a job whose effort differs from `runtime.effort` shows it as a chip on its card; see [Effort](CONFIG.md#effort). When it is in use, the status bar's **Daemon** indicator summarises the pool (with `foreman.enabled`, the **Foreman** indicator before it opens the live thread of the office session and its assistant foremen, which decide parked gates), and its popover holds the rest: its status, `live/cap` job sessions, a Start/Stop toggle, a row per session with its state (held on budget, waiting for a slot, kept by running agents and so on) that opens a job's log, and the inbox session's live log (the **Inbox session log** toggle, in the Daemon popover). Every pending permission prompt is answered in the **Needs you** popover. `forge daemon status` and `forge daemon log <job> --follow` show the same from a terminal.
