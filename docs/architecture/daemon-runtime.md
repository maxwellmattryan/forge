# Daemon runtime

**Files** (`.forge/runtime/daemons/`, gitignored, unversioned): `<scope>.json` holds `{sessionId, resumable, pid, serverPid, argv0, startedAt, spawnSessionId, lastTurnAt, logBase}`. `<scope>.log` gets every child stdout line verbatim, except that a JSON line with neither claude's `timestamp` (only its `assistant` and `user` lines carry one) nor an `at` gets the receipt time as `at`, so every line has a time; non-JSON stdout as `forge-raw`, stderr as `forge-stderr` (4 KiB per line), and the supervisor's own events as `forge-daemon` (`spawn`, `turn` (with `requests` and the session's turn number), `result`, `exit`, `stop`, `idle-stop`, `waiting`, `finished`, `resume-failed`, `session-changed`, `held`, `retry` (with `at`, `reason`, `attempt`, `requests`), `reaped`, `error`, `line-truncated`, `wake` (a turn claude started by itself), `agents-lost` (with `job` and `roles`), `lease-expired` (with `job`, `session` and `reason`), `requeued` (with `requests` and `rejected`), `requests-closed` (with `requests`: the `resume` requests a working turn written for them took up), `reconcile` (with `action`), `turn-timeout` (with `minutes`), `meter` (with `usd`, `tokens` and `turn`, one per metered result), `rotated` (with `tokens` and `threshold`, once per child: see **Rotation**); `stop` and `idle-stop` carry a `reason`). A stdout line over 8 MiB is dropped and replaced by a `line-truncated` marker. Each session rotates its own log to `<scope>.log.1` at spawn once it passes 20 MiB or, with `retention.logMaxBytes` set, as soon as it reaches that size mid-session (one older generation is kept, logBase grows by the rotated size, and writes still pending on the old descriptor land in `.log.1`, which is why the rotated size counts them); `GET /api/daemons/log` pages from `.log` back into `.log.1` under the same confinement. It pauses its child's stdout while the log stream is full. Pool-wide events (a trust stop, a config refusal) are written to every live session's log. A chat's session keeps these files under its `_chat-<id>` scope like any session, and its spend goes to `.forge/runtime/meter/_chat-<id>.jsonl`; the chat itself, `chat.json` and its `log.jsonl` history, lives in `.forge/runtime/chats/<id>/` ([Daemon](daemon.md) › Chats). Gate runs have no session record: forge dash's gate runner keeps one JSON-lines gate log, `_gates.log` (`gate-start`, `gate-end`, `gate-held` and `error` lines), and meters into `.forge/runtime/meter/_gates.jsonl`.

**Offsets.** Every log line has an offset: `logBase` plus the line's byte position in `<scope>.log`. A rotation adds the rotated file's size to `logBase`, so offsets only grow. Offsets are the SSE ids and the history keys, so the dashboard merges live lines and older pages without duplicates. The last 200 lines of each session stay in memory for SSE replay.

**Session and resume.** Each scope keeps its own session id. The inbox (`_inbox`) and the office (`_office`) never resume: every spawn (after an idle, slot or human stop, a failed start, a restarted `forge dash`) gets a new `--session-id <uuid>`, whatever the record's `resumable` says, so neither carries a conversation that grows without bound. Job and chat sessions resume: the first spawn gets a fresh `--session-id <uuid>`; once a child of that session has written a line, `resumable` is set and later spawns (a restarted `forge dash`, new work after an idle or slot stop) use `--resume <uuid>`, passing `--plugin-dir` again because resume does not restore it, until a job session rotates (below). A `system`/`init` with another `session_id` replaces the stored one (`session-changed`).

**Rotation.** A job session retires its conversation once it grows past `daemon.freshAfterTokens` (default 200000; `null` never rotates), so a long job's every resume does not rewrite hundreds of thousands of tokens into the prompt cache; its next child rebuilds its state from `forge job step`. At every `result` (working or failed) the stream reports the turn's **context**: the `input_tokens + cache_read_input_tokens + cache_creation_input_tokens` of the turn's last main-loop `assistant` line (a subagent's line, with a non-empty `parent_tool_use_id`, never counts; neither does the cumulative cost the meter reads), each count finite and ≥ 0 or else 0; a turn with no such line has no reading and changes nothing. The first time a child's turn context is more than the threshold, the session sets `resumable: false` in its record (so whatever stops this child next, the next spawn gets a new session id), logs `rotated` with `tokens` and `threshold`, and is due to stop. At that result and every later one, after the meter and the post-turn confine check, it stops at the first safe boundary: the child still runs, the session is `idle`, no running agent of its job keeps it (the `kept` rule), and claude's last `background_tasks_changed` reported no task. Until then the conversation keeps running; a background agent's end wakes the orchestrator, and that wake turn's result checks again (the `background` event itself never stops it: claude's wake `init` follows it at once). The log then reads `rotated`, `stop` with `reason: "rotate"`, `exit`, `lease-expired` with `reason: "rotate"` and `requeued`: the usual release, with no agents lost since none ran. The session stays wanted, so its next work (a new request, or the reconciler's resume of the job whose lease expired) spawns a child on a new conversation, metered from a baseline of 0. The inbox, office and chat sessions never rotate. A `--resume` child that exits non-zero without ever emitting `system`/`init` lost its transcript: the supervisor logs `resume-failed`, undoes whatever that child's error `result` committed, starts one fresh session and re-ticks, without counting a failure.

**Failures** are per session. A spawn `error` with no pid (ENOENT, EACCES) stops wanting that session (`cannot start daemon: …`), with no retry. Unexpected exits with no `result` since spawn are counted; at 3 the session stops wanting itself (`daemon stopped after 3 failed starts: …`); any `result` resets the count. `idleStopMinutes` ends stdin and stops an idle child, keeping the session wanted. The window counts from the later of the last spawn or `result` and the last agent end, and never runs out while a running agent keeps the session. **Lost agents:** whenever a job session's child exits, for any reason (pool or session stop, `forge dash` shutdown, a trust change or other pool-wide stop, a permission-mode narrowing, failed-turn strike-out, a job-finished or idle stop, a crash), every agent still marked `running` on a job one of its ids drives is ended with status `lost` and summary `lost: its daemon session ended`, through the same `recordAgent` path as `forge job agent <id> end --status lost` (an `agent-end` job event), and the session logs one `agents-lost` with `job` and `roles` per job. It happens before the stop resolves. Agents that already ended, and another owner's agents, are never touched, so the next session's `step` respawns the role (L1) or retries the reviewer (M1) instead of waiting on phantoms (G5). A job that finishes (`done` or `cancelled`) with an agent still marked `running` ends it the same way in the write that finishes it, with summary `lost: the job was <state> while it ran` and an `agent-end` event, and a job an older Forge finished that way reads as `lost` (no event), so nothing waits on an agent a finished job can never end. A failed `job.json` read or write here is logged as an `error` and the exit still completes. The synchronous last-resort SIGTERM from an `exit` handler marks nothing; the next orchestrator handles those agents as before. A `result` with `is_error` (claude reports an expired login or an exhausted quota that way, often with subtype `success`) means no request made progress: the child stays up, nothing is committed, and the session's `lastError` reads `last turn failed: …; retry at <time>` until a turn works or the session is stopped on purpose, so the dashboard shows it failing rather than idle. The session writes no turn before that time, which a `retry` log line records with its `reason` and `attempt`: when the child printed a `rate_limit_event` with `status: "rejected"` during the turn, the retry is at that window's `resetsAt`; otherwise it is a backoff that doubles from 1 minute (5 minutes for a limit without a reset time, such as an API key's spend cap) to 1 hour. A failure is a `limit` when that event was seen or the message says so (usage or rate limit, 429, 529, overloaded): limits are retried for as long as it takes. Any other failure is a strike, and at 3 in a row the session stops wanting itself (`daemon stopped after 3 failed turns: …`) and stops its child so the slot frees. A `result` whose `subtype` is `error_max_budget_usd` (the child reached the `--max-budget-usd` cap its spawn passed, logged as `capUsd` in the `spawn` line) is a failed turn whatever `is_error` says, never a limit, and stops the session at once the same way, with a `lastError` naming the cap and the child stopped with reason `budget-cap`. A working turn clears the strikes and the backoff, as does a human's Start. In every case the other sessions keep running and the pool stays wanted; a session that isn't wanted spawns nothing until a human starts it (or the whole pool) again. What is repo-wide stops the whole pool and every child, with the error on the pool and in every session log: a trust-digest change, `daemon.enabled: false` at spawn, a `daemon.*` config problem, and symlinks under `.forge/trackers/`.

**Start and stop.** A pool start (dashboard Start, or `forge dash` booting with `enabled: true`) refuses while another live `forge dash` owns any record; otherwise it wants the pool, resets every session's wanted flag, failures and error, takes a new trust snapshot only when no child runs anywhere, and ticks. Stop stops every child. One session can be started (only inside a wanted pool, and never with a new snapshot) or stopped from its job panel's header (the Inbox and office sessions from their logs); a stopped job session stays listed and spawns nothing for new work until started again.

**Effort.** At every spawn, a session resolves its effort with `resolveEffort` (`lib/core/config/models.ts`), the one place levels combine. A session runs the orchestrator, so it resolves with no role: its job's `job.effort`, else `runtime.effort`, from the config bytes that spawn just hashed and the `job.json` read then. The inbox uses `runtime.effort` alone, and so does a session whose job is missing or unreadable. A level adds `--effort <level>` to argv after Forge's `--permission-mode` and before `--disallowedTools`, replacing every `--effort x` and `--effort=x` in `daemon.command`; with none, Forge passes nothing and the template's own `--effort`, if any, applies. The log records `{"event":"effort","level":<level> | "inherit","source":"job" | "config" | "none"}` right after `spawn` (a session never gets the `panel`, `role` or `complexity` sources that `resolveEffort` gives agents), and the session's `effort` in `/api/state` is the level its running child got (null while none runs, or when Forge passed none). A changed effort never stops, signals or messages a running child: it applies at that session's next spawn (an idle stop, a slot handover, a human's stop and start). Agents inherit their session's effort, since no `forge:*` agent's frontmatter sets one, and also get the level explicitly: every `spawn` from `forge job step` carries `effort` and every brief an `Effort:` line, which the orchestrator passes as the Agent tool's `effort`. An agent's level is resolved for its role: `job.effort`, the lens's panel `effort`, `runtime.efforts.<role>`, `runtime.effortByComplexity[job.complexity]`, then `runtime.effort`, and a builder on round 2 or later gets one level more unless the job's own effort set it. So agents started after a change already run at the new level while the session itself switches at its next spawn.

**HTTP surface** (consumed by the dashboard; every route below is a project route, served at `/api/p/<project>/…`). `/api/p/<project>/state` has `config.daemon: {enabled, jobBudget, billing}` (`billing` is `{ vars, passed, warn }` for the API keys, provider switches and `ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL` set in `forge dash`'s environment, which sessions inherit, or null when none is; `warn` is true when a provider switch or `ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL` is among them, `forge doctor`'s rule; the Workers popover shows a line while it is non-null, in warning tone only when `warn`), `config.effort` and `daemons: {enabled, wanted, maxConcurrent, live, lastError, needsApproval, inbox, office, foreman, gates, jobs, clients}` (`foreman` is `foreman.enabled` from the latest config read; `gates` is the gate runner's `{enabled, parallel, live, held, runs}`, `runs` being the live gate runs and the 50 latest, newest first, each `{request, gate, job, mandate, status, outcome, reason, startedAt, endedAt, attempts, usd, tokens}`; `needsApproval` is true while the machine dashboard keeps this project's pool off until a human presses Start, see ["Daemon security"](daemon-security.md)), and each job row has `gateOwner` (`foreman`, `human`, or `null` when it waits on nothing or the foreman is off), where `inbox`, `office` and each `jobs[id]` are `{scope, wanted, running, status, sessionId, pid, lastTurnAt, lastResultAt, turns, failures, held, waiting, lastError, permissions, permissionMode, kept, effort, contextTokens}` (`kept` is `"agents"` while a child runs, the session isn't `stopping` and a running agent keeps it, else `null`; `effort` is the `--effort` Forge passed the running child, else `null`; `contextTokens` is the running child's latest turn context (**Rotation** above), `null` while no child runs or before its first reading, for every session kind; the Workers popover appends it to a job session's row as ` · 245k context`); `jobs` lists a session while it runs or stops, waits, is held, has an error, or was stopped by a human. `GET /api/events?session=<scope>` streams that session's `event: state` on connect (an off state for an unknown session) and on every change, its buffered lines after `Last-Event-ID`, then `event: line` with `id: <offset>` per new line (lines over 64 KiB become a `forge-truncated` marker), and `: ping` every 15 s; lines of other sessions are never sent. `GET /api/daemons/log?session=<scope>&before=<offset>` returns a page of complete lines read backwards from `before` (at most 256 KiB or 1000 lines, lines over 64 KiB as markers at their real offset, at most 16 MiB scanned) as `{scope, lines: [{offset, line}], start, end, more}`; only the current `<scope>.log` is served. `POST /api/daemon {"action":"start"|"stop", "scope"?}` answers 200 with the pool state; without a scope it starts or stops the pool (409 `{error}` for `start` while `daemon.enabled` is false or the `daemon.*` config has a problem), with one it starts or stops that session (400 for an invalid scope, 404 for an unknown job, 409 `start the daemon first` for a scoped start while the pool isn't wanted). `POST /api/requests` answers with the request plus `daemon: <pool wanted>`, and 400 for a `gate` request (only the CLI writes those), for a `run` without a valid `args.ref`, an `args.link` outside `approve` or not `new`, `none` or a ref, or an `args.n` outside `next` or 1..16. `POST /api/jobs {text, title?}` creates a job (title derived from the text unless given; 400 for missing, blank or over-20,000-character text or an empty title, 409 before `/forge init`), queues its `resume`, and answers `{id, title, branch, request, daemon}`. `POST /api/jobs/<id>/effort {"effort": "low" | "medium" | "high" | "xhigh" | "max" | null}` sets or (null) clears the job's effort through the same `setJobEffort` as `forge job effort`, and answers `{id, effort, previous, changed}`; it works while the pool is wanted, because `job.json` is outside the trust digest, and is 400 for any other body and 404 for an unknown id or `_inbox`. `POST /api/jobs/<id>/repos {"decision": "approve" | "reject", "repos": [names], "note"?: string | null}` decides a job's pending repo proposals through `decideRepoProposals` with the dashboard as actor, and answers `{id, decision, repos, added, pending, resumed}`; when nothing is left pending and the job sits on the RP1 park it sets the job running and queues a `resume` unless a live session holds it (`resumed`). It is 404 for an unknown id, 400 for any other body (a `repos` that isn't a non-empty list of repo names, or a note over 2,000 characters), and 409 when nothing is pending, when `repos` is not exactly the pending set, or when a clone check fails. `GET /api/jobs/<id>` includes the job's `requests` (every status, oldest first, the last 50) and `request`, its `request.md` without the title line (at most 2,000 characters), which opens the drawer's Thread, with `requestTruncated` true when it was cut (the Thread's **Show the whole request** then reads `request.md` through `GET /api/jobs/<id>/file`), and `requestFromTracker`, true only when the job's `created` event carries an issue with a ref (its `issues`, or a pre-v11 `source`; a job started from an issue), so a job linked later still shows `request` as the user's words.

**Terminal.** `forge daemon status` prints the dashboard and every session record from the runtime files (no server call). `dashboard` comes from the machine dashboard's record, `<FORGE_HOME>/dashboard.json`, else this repo's legacy `.forge/runtime/dashboard.json` (an older per-repo `forge dash`). It is `{pid, url, alive: true}` only when that record is proven to name a running `forge dash` (["Daemon security"](daemon-security.md)); a record that names an integer pid but fails the proof is `{pid, url: null, alive: false}`, and no record (or no integer pid) is `null`. Each session has `{scope, job, pid, alive, sessionId, startedAt, lastTurnAt, logBytes, kept, agents}`: `agents` lists the sorted roles of the running agents of the job that session owns (read from the record's `sessionId` and the job's `job.json`), and `kept` is `"agents"` when one of them keeps it; both are `null` and `[]` when the session is not alive, is the inbox or the office, or owns no agents. The office sorts right after the inbox, then the jobs. `forge daemon log <job>|--inbox|--office|--gates [--lines N] [--follow]` (combining two of `--inbox`, `--office` and `--gates`, or `--gates` with a job, is a usage error) prints a session log's tail, or with `--gates` the gate log's, as raw JSONL with every C0/C1 control character escaped, and `--follow` keeps printing new lines across rotations.

**Stopping and orphans.** Children are spawned `detached` with no shell, so each leads its own process group. A stop SIGTERMs the group and SIGKILLs it after 5 s. `forge dash` stops every session on SIGTERM/SIGINT before exiting, and an `exit` handler sends a synchronous SIGTERM to each as a last resort. `forge dash --stop` signals the server only when `dashboard.json` is proven to name it (["Daemon security"](daemon-security.md)) and waits up to 12 s for it; a stale record is cleared without signalling anything, so `stopped` is false. Either way it then reaps (`daemonReaped: true` when anything was). A pool being created also reaps. Each record (every `daemons/<scope>.json`, regular files with a valid scope name only) is proven on its own: if it names a live pid whose server is gone, the pid is signalled only after it is proven to be the recorded child: it is > 1 and not us or our group, it leads its own process group, `ps` reports a start time within 30 s of `startedAt`, its command line contains the recorded `claude` executable and `--session-id`/`--resume` followed by the recorded uuid. A live `serverPid` other than ours in any record means another dashboard owns the daemon, and a pool start refuses. None of this runs on Windows, so on native Windows a pool start is refused outright (`lastError` says why and links [SUPPORT.md](../SUPPORT.md)), nothing is spawned, and the dashboard serves without the daemon; `forge dash` prints the refusal (`daemonRefused` under `--background`) and skips the `daemon.auth` question.

**Role runs.** `forge job agent run <id> --session S [--spawn-index N] [--epoch N]` (lib/cli/commands/job/agent-run.ts) runs one spawn `forge job step` returned as its own headless child, a **role run**, instead of a subagent of the job's session; its child half, `runRoleChild` in lib/integrations/claude/role.ts, knows nothing of jobs. Nothing calls it automatically yet. *Before anything is written* it checks the flags, the job, the scope (`refuseInboxDrive`, and under `FORGE_DAEMON=1` only the job's own session: the child carries that job's scope), that S holds the lease (and `--epoch`), that step still returns a spawn with index N in `[action, ...spawns]` (else `stale_step`), the config read once (`trackerSymlinks`, `daemonProblems`, and under `FORGE_DAEMON=1` a `FORGE_DAEMON_TRUST` equal to the digest of those bytes), the worktree (`jobWorktreeProblem`, and under one of the write roots' worktrees roots) and the brief. *The start* is `forge job agent start`'s path (role, model, `--output`, `--retry`), fenced by `{epoch, session}` plus a check that re-runs step under the job lock, so of two callers racing for one spawn exactly one starts. *argv* is `roleArgv` (lib/integrations/claude/argv.ts): `daemon.command`'s first word, then `-p --input-format stream-json --output-format stream-json --verbose --plugin-dir <plugin> --agent forge:<role> --setting-sources '' --strict-mcp-config --disable-slash-commands --tools <tools>` (`''` is one empty argument: no setting source loads, neither the worktree's own `CLAUDE.md`, `.claude/settings*.json` and `.mcp.json`, which are files the branch's agents wrote, nor the user's settings, plugins and hooks, none of them Forge's, so only the plugin and managed settings load; `<tools>` is the built-in tools of the agent's `tools:` frontmatter, read by `agentTools` in lib/engine/pipeline/roster.ts, its `mcp__…` entries dropped, every name matching `^[A-Z][A-Za-z]+$`, and an agent without the list refuses the run before anything is written), `--model` and `--effort` when valid, `--append-system-prompt-file <file>` when the run has a trusted prefix (below), `--mcp-config <json>` when the spawn gets Forge's browser (below), `--permission-mode` from the template, `--allowedTools` with every file tool swapped for one `Edit(//<root>/**)` per write root (and `mcp__forge-browser` last with the browser, since no one can answer a role child's prompt), `--session-id <uuid>` or `--resume <uuid>`, `--disallowedTools` with the absolute deny rules only (`absoluteDenyRules`, lib/office/session/confine.ts), and `--permission-prompt-tool stdio`; the template's other words are ignored. *The browser* is `browserMcpConfig` (lib/integrations/claude/browser.ts): one `forge-browser` server, `npx -y @playwright/mcp@<exact version> --headless --isolated --allowed-origins http://localhost:<port>;http://127.0.0.1:<port> --output-dir <job dir>/screenshots`, built from the job's integer `port` and a Forge path only. A role run passes it when `needsBrowser` (lib/engine/pipeline/brief.ts) holds, for the builder, the designer or a reviewer whose lens is `ui`, `ux`, `accessibility` or `design`, while the spec has a UI AC (`specUiAcs`, the builder's `UI proof:` ACs), and the job has a port; a job session's spawn passes it under the same spec and port rule (`sessionBrowser`, lib/office/slots.ts), with its `allowedTools` unchanged, so browser calls are prompts as other MCP calls are; the inbox, office, chat and gate-run sessions never get it. Without it a role has no browser, and when `npx` or Chrome is missing the server fails to start and the role runs without browser tools. *The trusted prefix* (lib/engine/guard/prompt-prefix.ts) is one file, `.forge/runtime/prompt-prefix/<sha256>.md`, holding a fixed head and then the main checkout's copies of the role's `project.instructions` files, `.forge/notes/learnings.md` and `.forge/notes/<role>.md`, those that exist, in that order: no job id, round, path, lens or checklist, so every lens of a panel, the next round and the next job get the same bytes and the same path, and claude's prompt cache serves them instead of writing them again (#374 measured the shared prefix rise from 15,062 to 23,910 tokens). It is built before the start, reused when a regular file with those bytes is there and otherwise replaced atomically, and it goes on the first launch, the resume and the fresh-session fallback alike. With it, the brief's `Instructions:` line becomes the fixed `PREFIX_INSTRUCTIONS_LINE` (inserted after `Effort:` when the brief had none), and every other line stays. A refused source (a link, a non-regular file, more than 256 KiB, a linked parent directory, an instruction entry under `.forge/` or a write root), a prefix over 256 KiB, a write that fails or a prefixed brief over the cap run the role exactly as before, with no flag and the brief unchanged. No source at all is no prefix and no refusal. *env* is `sessionEnv` (`FORGE_DAEMON=1`, `FORGE_MODE=agent`, the git pins, no API key or provider switch unless `daemon.auth: api-key`), plus `FORGE_ROOT` (pinned to the main checkout, as the pool pins it for every session), `FORGE_DAEMON_WRITE_ROOTS`, `FORGE_DAEMON_SCOPE=<id>` and `FORGE_DAEMON_TRUST` (the caller's own under `FORGE_DAEMON=1`, else the digest just taken: whoever runs the CLI outside a session is the trust anchor). *cwd* is the realpath of the job's worktree, the path that was checked. *stdin* gets the brief as one stream-json user line (`briefLine`, at most 200,000 characters), and stays open for permission answers; with no one there, every `can_use_tool` request is denied with the fixed `UNATTENDED_DENIED` text and counted as `denied`. When claude's `init` reports a uuid session id the runner records `agent attach` with it, so the Thread view and activity line follow the run. *The end*: after the `result` line the runner closes stdin, waits 10 s, then SIGTERMs the child's process group and SIGKILLs it 5 s later (`daemon.turnTimeoutMinutes` bounds a run that never reaches a result; SIGINT or SIGTERM to the runner stops the child first). It records `forge job agent end`'s path (the output check, the secret scan, budget enforcement, lease renewal) fenced by `{epoch}`, so a session that took the job over meanwhile refuses it (`stale_epoch`, the run log kept): `done` with the result's first non-empty line as the summary (redacted of the job's known secrets), or `failed` with `<class>: <reason>`, the class one of `limit` (with `resetsAt`), `auth`, `budget`, `error`, `timeout`, `untrusted` (the trust digest or the confine digest, the one a session's turns are held to, changed during the run), `interrupted` (exit 1), `exit` or `spawn` (exit 5). Step's L1 rule respawns a failed role. The JSON also carries `prefix`, the trusted prefix file passed or `null`, and `prefixRefused`, the fixed reason naming the source's checkout-relative path, when a refusal made the run go without one. *Usage* is the result's `modelUsage` summed into one exact AgentUsage, under the model that used the most tokens. A child that started but sent no cost (a timeout, a kill, an exit before the result) is measured from its session transcript as a resume without a sidecar is, and flagged `unmeasured` when there is none. *Resume*: a spawn whose `resume` is a uuid gets `--resume`, and its usage is the new cumulative less the baseline that session's sidecar holds; without a sidecar it falls back to importing the session transcript (`<projects>/<slug(worktree)>/<uuid>.jsonl`), else `unmeasured`. A resume that fails before `init` is retried once as a fresh session (`resumeFailed: true`); a `resume` that is not a uuid starts fresh. *Files* (`.forge/runtime/agents/<id>/`, gitignored): `<session>.log` gets every stdout and stderr line redacted of the job's known secrets, plus the runner's own `forge-permission` lines, and `<session>.json` is the sidecar `{v: 1, cum, at}` with the last cumulative cost, written after every result. Both are opened through dirs created a level at a time and never through a link.

**Lean sessions.** What a session or role child loads is read from claude's own `system`/`init` line. The probe, in a scratch git repo (with `FORGE_ROOT` and `FORGE_HOME` pointed away from real projects): `claude -p --model haiku --output-format stream-json --verbose --setting-sources '' --strict-mcp-config --disable-slash-commands --tools Read,Write --plugin-dir <plugin> "reply ok"`; add `--agent forge:<role>` and that agent's `--tools` for a role child, or swap the four lean flags for the default `daemon.command`'s for a session. Read the init's `plugins`, `mcp_servers`, `tools`, `skills` and `slash_commands`.
