# Foreman: authority, audit and threat model

Part of [Foreman](../foreman.md).

## Authority

Only the gate runner records a foreman decision, a foreman release approval, a foreman milestone-draft decision or a Foreman hand-off: it holds a capability object (`issueForemanAuthority()`, a frozen object registered in a module-private `WeakSet`), and no argv, environment or parsed text can produce one. `checkForemanDecision`, `checkMandateGateDecision` and `checkReleaseDecision` take it as `by` and refuse anything else first (`--actor foreman refused: only the Foreman's gate runner in forge dash records foreman decisions`); every check runs before any write, and a refusal leaves `job.json` and `events.jsonl` untouched.

From the CLI, `forge job human … --actor foreman` (`approve-release` included) and `forge mandate draft approve|revise --actor foreman` therefore exit 1 in every scope, a person's terminal included. `forge job handoff` no longer exists. `forge job assist` and `forge mandate draft assist` exit 1 in every daemon session and stay read-only views for a person's own terminal. In the office (scope `_office`, where an office run's children run) any `forge job human` exits 1 (`the office records no gate decisions: forge dash's gate runner decides gates`), and `approve-repos`/`reject-repos` exit 1 in every daemon session. A `FORGE_DAEMON_SCOPE` of `_gates` refuses every command (`forge refuses: a gate run's assistant runs no forge commands`), and a retired assistant session's scope is an invalid scope.

With the authority, a decision needs a note, takes no `--link` or `--reason`, and needs the job to be waiting on a class in `foreman.decides` (`pr-review` counts as `pr-feedback`) with an action of that class: `approve-spec`/`revise-spec`, `approve-split`/`revise-split`, `approve`/`revise-spec`/`rework` for escalation and rounds-exhausted, `approve` for conflicts, `rework` for pr-feedback, `approve-repos`/`reject-repos` for repo-proposal, naming exactly the pending set its assist judged.

It is refused when a `foreman.reserved` pattern matches the reason, the note, or (for escalation and rounds-exhausted) the `## Why` and `## For the human` sections of `decision.md`, or (for repo-proposal) a pending proposal's reason; a `decision.md` that isn't a small regular file counts as a match, and a security review whose latest recorded verdict is anything but `PASS` (`CHANGES`, `MALFORMED`) refuses every action that accepts the job, at any gate, while `rework` and the revise actions stay the foreman's (`SECURITY_MITIGATIONS`): security always has to pass, so the foreman may fix it but never approve past it.

Step keeps reworking such a job past `maxRounds`, up to twice it, before B2 parks it on `rounds-exhausted`. Past the **total-round ceiling** (`pipeline.maxTotalRounds`, by default twice `maxRounds`), which counts every round whoever opened it, B7 parks the job there unless a person (a `human` event whose actor isn't the foreman) reworked or revised since the previous round started, and every foreman action at that park is refused, whatever the security verdict: no gate request is queued for it. A `rework` on pr-feedback also needs a pending or in-progress `gate` request keyed by the PR's current head sha, so the foreman acts only on feedback sync armed for that push.

Patterns match case-insensitively as whole phrases (no letter or digit on either side) and are escaped literals, so matching is linear; a `reserved` that isn't a list of non-empty strings makes every gate reserved.

In the office, `forge job set` only carries out a recorded foreman decision: the job must still wait, its latest `human` event must be the foreman's and newer than the park, and the change must be that action's `--state running` with its stage from the gate table, with no `--field`, `--waiting-on` or `--reason`. Of the `forge job` subcommands only `assist`, `human`, `set`, `note`, `show`, `get`, `list` and `step` run there (and `assist` and `human` then refuse). The office drives no job: `worktree create`, `job advance`, `job claim`, `job agent … start`, `pr merge` (any job, with or without `--now`) and `backing down --shared` are refused with `the office does not drive jobs`.

The assistant has no Bash at all (`disallowedTools: Bash, Edit, NotebookEdit`, and a gate run denies Bash again), so text it reads reaches the decision only through its one-line verdict, which the policy checks judge like any other note.

## Audit

Every new `human` event carries `actor: { login, source }` (["Audit trail"](../audit-trail.md)); a foreman decision is `{ login: null, source: "foreman" }`, and an older event's `"foreman"` reads as that, its `"human"` or missing actor as `{ login: null, source: "unknown" }`. `isForemanActor` (lib/core/job/store.ts) is the one check protected paths, stats, retro, the title-only rule and the office's `forge job set` use, and it accepts the legacy string too. `forge stats` counts only human reworks in `humanReworks` and adds `foremanDecisions` and `foremanReworks`.

The dashboard labels foreman decisions in the drawer's timeline, shows each waiting job's `gateOwner` on its card, and shows the office runs and the gate runs in the status bar as the **Foreman**: an indicator always rendered first in the left cluster (active while an office run or any gate run is live, with no prompts since no run asks anyone, or dashed while `foreman.enabled` is off) whose chat-sized popover shows the identity block, the office runs' daily-cap hold, then the **gate activity list** from `/api/state`'s `gates` (`GateRunsState`): a header with `<live> of <parallel> running` and any pause (the daily cap's message, or until when a retry or limit holds), and one row per run, newest first (the 50 kept), with the gate, its job (which opens the drawer) or mandate, an outcome chip, the reason on one line, when it started and its tokens, the dollars only in a title.

Every value renders as text. The Workers popover leads with a Foreman row that opens it, and has no office pill. The Office page's right column lists the office runs (type, mandate, outcome, reason, start, tokens).

## Threat model

A session's provenance is `FORGE_DAEMON_SCOPE`, unauthenticated as for the inbox, but no scope carries the Foreman's authority: the checks stop a session misled by request, PR or tracker text (a job, inbox, office or chat session passing a decision off as the foreman's, the assistant smuggling a reserved topic through its reason, a forged `gate` request with `reserved: null`, the office merging), not a hostile local process.

**Human-only gate reasons:** at an `escalation` park whose reason lists `protected-paths`, `lens-escalate` or `runbook`, or while the job has a protected-paths flag no person approved whatever the reason now says, `reservedFor` holds every action (`(escalation: <rule> needs a person)`), so `checkForemanDecision` refuses approve and rework, `gateOwner` says `human`, the gate request is queued `reserved` and handed off with no child, and a chat is refused; step never counts a Foreman approve for those rules either, so a Foreman approve can't lead to an auto-merge past them. The Foreman still decides a park whose rules are only `approver`, `acs`, `version` or `dependencies`; its brief carries the same `Gate reasons (JSON)` line as the approver's. A Bash-capable session can always run `FORGE_DAEMON= forge job human …` without `--actor foreman`, which reads as a person outside the daemon: the CLI line guards a misled model, not a hostile one.

A release approval that outlives its mandate stops counting at run and status time, and tampering with the mandate file only makes it unreadable, which revokes it. A CI rework on a job whose mandate keeps `pr-feedback` with a human becomes the gate instead, with the mandate read from the CLI's record of the job, never `job.json`.

## Gate run threat model

For gate runs, the guard that moved into code is who may record a foreman decision or hand-off, plus the path by which brief text reaches a claude stdin. The attackers, and how each bypass fails:
1. A daemon model session (job, inbox, office, chat) misled by tracker, PR or request text into recording a foreman decision or hand-off: the CLI refuses `--actor foreman` in every scope and `forge job handoff` is gone, because the authority is an in-process object no argv or environment can produce. A Bash-capable session setting `FORGE_DAEMON_SCOPE` to a retired assistant session's scope gets an invalid scope, and `_gates` refuses every command. Residual, as before: a hostile local process can import the module itself; the line guards a misled model.
2. The `forge:assistant` misled by job text: its only outputs are the verdict line and `feedback.md`; the parser is strict and cleans the reason; the note still goes through `reservedFor`; it has no Bash and its `_gates` scope refuses every `forge` call; and `dontAsk` means no prompt ever reaches a person to approve.
3. Verdict-line games: several `ASSIST:` lines (the last counts), text after the line, a lookalike dot or a typo are malformed, and a malformed reply never becomes approve.
4. A forged gate request file (from a Bash-capable session) with `reserved: null` or a wrong key: assist recomputes reserved, the policy recomputes everything, and pr-feedback needs the request keyed by the current head. Job ids and gate classes are validated before any path or argv is built.
5. Brief text on stdin: the brief is the CLI-built text (paths, the gate class, config lists, mandate guidance fenced by `fenceImport`), JSON-encoded into one user line by `briefLine`, so it can't become a control line, and refused over 256 KiB.
6. Spend and concurrency amplification: `foreman.assistants.parallel` is 1–16 and locked; the office cap covers `_gates` and fails closed on an unreadable meter; each child is capped at the remainder; failures back off; a request is requeued once, then rejected.
7. A planted `_gates.log` (a link, a FIFO or a forged `gate-end`): it is written with `openAppend` and read with `readLogPage` (no-follow), each seeded record is validated, and the dashboard renders text only.
8. Loops: one retry per run, one counted requeue per request, and one title-only revise per person's decision.
9. The senior pass (`foreman.escalations: foreman`). The guard is still who may record a foreman decision, now plus who may shelve as the Foreman, and how the first assistant's text reaches the senior:
   - The first assistant, steered by job text, smuggling instructions to the senior through its reason: the reason is cleaned (no quotes, backticks, `$`, `\`, control or line separators, at most 200 characters) and JSON-encoded on one line labelled data, and the whole brief still goes through `briefLine`.
   - The first assistant, steered by job text, naming an injection attempt and so sending the same text to a second model: a first reason that names `injection` is shelved for a person before any senior pass ([check 4](gates.md#senior-pass)), as a detected injection always reached a person under `human`.
   - A model-written reason reaching a public tracker comment (the assistant has unscoped `Read`, so a steered one could put a file's text in its reason): the Foreman's shelve posts fixed text and the gate's name, and the full reason stays on the job, its events and the gate log, which are local.
   - The senior, steered by job text or the diff, approving past a floor: its decision goes through `recordForemanDecision` (the authority, the gate's actions, `reservedFor` on its note, the security floor, mandate holds, the pr-feedback head key), and security and reserved are screened before it starts, so a security hold is shelved for a person and never judged.
   - A model session shelving or forging a senior decision through the CLI: only the in-process authority shelves as the Foreman (`recordForemanShelve`), and `forge job shelve` stays refused in every daemon session.
   - Loops and spend: one marker per park, written before the spawn, survives a restart; each child is capped at the office remainder, and the cap is checked again before the senior.
   - A forged or deleted marker in the appendable `events.jsonl`: a forged one only shelves sooner, toward a person; a deleted one allows at most one more senior pass per run, still under every check above. Residual, as for every events-derived guard: the line guards a misled model, not a hostile local process.
