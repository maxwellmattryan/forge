Forge is a software factory for AI coding agents: you hand it a feature, a bug or a backlog item; a team of agents plans, specs, builds, reviews and opens a pull request, or stops and asks you. The dashboard is the smithy: a gunmetal floor (`bg`, grained, dark at its corners), plates of brushed steel standing on it (`panel`, grained, bevelled top and bottom, cut at two corners, seamed in `line`), the anvil at its side (`chrome`: the sidebar and the status bar), and one light. The gleam of polished steel is the machine at work: where the agents are running, the metal shines. Ember, the palette's one signal for a person, is where the work has come out of the fire and waits for your hand. Nothing else is coloured.

## Voice and content

- Write in plain, short sentences. Buttons are a verb, rarely two words: "Run next", "Approve", "Accept split", "Start", "Stop", "Follow", "Load older".
- Address the person as *you* and the system as *Forge* ("Forge v0.15.0" in the status bar, "Forge · {repo}" in the window title). Agents are named by role in lowercase mono: `planner`, `builder`, `reviewer`.
- Sentence case for prose and titles. Uppercase belongs to the technical styles only: `nav`, `control`, `label` and `stamp` are set in DM Mono with tracking, like letters struck into a plate, and `display` sets the name alone. Never uppercase a heading or body copy.
- Numbers, ids, refs, branches, verbs and paths are always mono (`mono`, `mono-log`): `job-7f3a`, `#142`, `LIN-88`, `forge/job-7f3a`, `/forge continue`.
- Smithy vocabulary is used sparingly and literally: `YOU` tags what waits on a person; a stamp line carries a measurement-like fact (`3 ACTIVE · BASE MAIN · GITHUB`, `V0.15.0 · API 3 · SHEET 1 OF 6`). Do not invent more: no "anvil", "hammer", "gleam" or "ember" in copy.
- Em-dash `—` is the empty value in tables and fact grids; a cold plate (dashed outline) is the empty state. Counts sit after their label in parentheses, in `muted` tabular numerals: "Active (3)", "Timeline (14)"; a count that stands alone at the right of a row (a nav badge, a chip) needs none.
- No emoji anywhere. Status is a word in a chip, never a glyph alone. Untrusted text (session output, tracker titles, request notes) renders as plain text, or as markdown only through the escape-first `md()` with a Raw view one click away.

## Colour

Three themes, the same smithy. Forge is the primary: a workshop at night, blue-grey steel lit by the gleam of polished metal; the only warm colour on the screen is ember, so a thing that needs you is unmistakable. Ash is the daylight theme: polished aluminium in daylight, a neutral-to-cool grey ground under whiter plates, the gleam a desaturated steel dark enough to read as text. Temper is the same smithy in the heat colours of tempered steel, and dark: a ground and plates of steel oxidised to indigo and violet, a peacock gleam, straw for `warn` and `spark`, patina green for `ok`, rose for `bad` and violet for `info`, with ember still the one signal for work that waits on you. Settings › System shows Ash on a light OS and Forge on a dark one; it never picks Temper, which is only ever chosen. Design in all three; every pair below is named in the tokens' usage notes.

- The ground is `bg`, gunmetal, with the steel grain over it and `bg-deep` shading the corners (the falloff). Only the ground carries the grain: Settings › Grain changes the ground and nothing else.
- Three surfaces step away from the ground. Plates (cards, KPI tiles, table wrappers, the drawer, dialogs, the command bar, popovers, toasts) fill with opaque `panel` inside a 1px `line` seam, with `shadow-bevel` inside them: a 1px `edge-light` along the top edge, a 1px `edge-dark` along the bottom, so every plate reads as forged, not drawn. The sidebar and the status bar are opaque `chrome`, the anvil, with a `line-strong` edge. `fg` is 15.58 → 13.91 → 16.47:1 on bg → panel → chrome in Forge, 14.22 → 15.99 → 16.87:1 in Ash and 15.15 → 13.34 → 16.36:1 in Temper; a plate stands at least 1.11:1 off the ground (1.120:1 Forge, 1.125:1 Ash, 1.136:1 Temper).
- Every fill is opaque. A filled control (a chip, a button, a nav item, a tab, a field, a progress track) takes the colour of the surface it sits on (`panel` in plates and overlays, `chrome` on the anvil, `bg` on the ground), so the grain never shows through it. `raised` is the one translucent wash (the active nav item, hovered table rows, the command bar's active option, inline code), always over a plain opaque fill.
- Three line strengths, one physical weight: `line` for every seam, `line-soft` for rules inside a plate, `line-strong` for the anvil's edge, the focus ring and a hovered card. Nothing is thicker than 1px except the 3px `bar-rule`: `gleam` down the active nav item and the command bar's active row, `ember` down a job that waits on you.
- Text is `fg` or `muted`; `dim` is for placeholders and stamp-line keys only (it does not meet 4.5:1 and is never used for copy a person must read).
- `gleam` is the shine on polished steel, a silver-blue that is never white (a desaturated steel in Ash, the peacock of tempered steel in Temper): the primary button, the selected segment, the progress fill, the active rule, links, the agent dot, the live dot, the `live` chip. A gleam fill is never flat: `--gleam-fill` (in `smithy.css`) is a brushed gradient lit from the upper left, a few percent of white at the top running to a few percent of `gleam-deep` at the bottom, with one thin specular line along the top edge (`--gleam-spec`); the progress bar brightens toward its end (`--gleam-fill-bar`) and the live dot is a small lit sphere. Text and lines in `gleam` stay solid. `on-gleam` is the text on it, never white or black. `gleam-hot` is the gleam caught by the light (a hovered primary button, the live dot's halo); `gleam-deep` is steel turned away from it, the lip under a primary button. There is no second accent.
- `ember` is the fire: the work has come out of it and waits for a person. The outline and left bar of a job waiting on you, the `YOU` chip, banners, the current stepper dot of a blocked or failed job, the settings rail's error dot. It is the palette's one signal for a person, so it is never decoration and never a fill under copy. In Forge and Ash it is the only warm colour; in Temper the heat colours sit beside it, so ember keeps its distance by hue in every theme: `warn`, `bad` and `spark` stand at least 12° of hue off it and `gleam`, `gleam-hot`, `ok` and `info` at least 60°, and Temper's ground, plates and chrome are never warm.
- `spark` is marker yellow and means *changed*: unsaved dots, new-row dots, the Thread tab's unread count, `::selection`; always a fill under `on-spark` text.
- Status hues each carry one meaning and always a word: `ok` success, `warn` attention, `bad` failure, `info` the neutral kind. Chips are the hue as text on a 10% tint of their surface inside a 45% outline of the same hue, never a solid fill (the `spark` chip is the one, and means changed); `warn` is yellow (straw in Temper) so it is never mistaken for `ember`, and `bad` leans salmon in Forge, brick in Ash and rose in Temper so it never rests on red-against-green alone.
- Glow belongs to the gleam, nowhere else: `shadow-glow` under the progress fill, the live dot and a hovered primary button. Overlays cast `shadow-overlay` (a plate lifted off the bench); the drawer `shadow-drawer`; the sidebar `shadow-chrome`. A card casts nothing: its bevel is its depth.
- Contrast: every text colour clears 4.5:1 on `bg`, `panel` and `chrome`, and on `panel` and `chrome` under `raised`, `sheen` or a hovered control's 12% gleam, in all three themes; `dim` is the stated exception (4.17:1 on `bg` in Forge, 3.17:1 in Ash, 3.88:1 in Temper). The weakest pairs: `info` on `panel` under `sheen` in Forge (4.72:1), `ember` on a hovered `panel` in Ash (4.95:1), `ember` on `panel` under `sheen` in Temper (4.64:1). The solid focus ring (`line-strong`, 2px at 2px) is at least 5.1:1 on every surface in every theme (5.59:1 Forge, 5.54:1 Ash, 6.32:1 Temper). Lines and glows are decorative and never the sole carrier of meaning: the thing a bar marks always has a word or a chip.

## Typography

Three families, self-hosted woff2 under `dashboard/fonts/` (the dashboard loads nothing cross-origin): Barlow Semi Condensed for headings, Barlow for reading, DM Mono for everything technical. One superfamily and one mono: the hierarchy comes from the switch between condensed, text and mono before it comes from size.

- Headings are Barlow Semi Condensed 600: `page-title` (32) for a view, `sheet-title` (22) for the drawer and dialogs, `section-title` (16) inside plates, `card-title` (15) for job cards and table rows. `display` (64, 700, uppercase) is the one stylistic setting and is reserved for the system's own name: the cover and an about screen, never the working UI.
- Body is Barlow 400 at `body` (14/20); rendered markdown uses `body-prose` (14/24) with the heading styles above and `mono` code on a `raised` ground. `caption` (12) carries timelines and footnotes.
- The technical layer is DM Mono, uppercase and tracked: `nav` (13, .04em) for nav and rail items, `control` (12, .06em) for buttons, segments and tabs, `label` (11, .10em, `muted`) for chips, column headers and callouts, `stamp` (10, .08em) for stamp lines and the status bar's facts. `mono` (12) and `mono-log` (11) are the only lowercase mono styles. `kpi-value` (28, 500, tabular) is the one large number.
- A heading in condensed over a mono label over Barlow copy is the standard stack of every plate.
- Numbers that line up are `tabular-nums`. Long titles clamp to two lines; mono facts `break-all`.

## Shape and surfaces

- Nothing is a perfect box. Every plate and control is cut at two opposite corners the way a smith trims sheet iron, with `corner-shape: bevel` so the cut is a straight chisel line (a browser without it shows a soft corner of the same size): `radius-cut` (10px) at a plate's top-left and bottom-right (a riveted plaque is the one plate with no cut), `radius-nick` (5px) on buttons and chips (top-left, bottom-right) and on inputs and nav items (top-right, bottom-left), so a control and the plate it sits on are never cut the same way. The other two corners keep the 2px `radius-1` chamfer. Segment strips keep only their outer nicks.
- Plates carry the same fine film grain as the ground (`--hammer`: the ground's noise at a 7% alpha, soft-light over `panel`) on a clipped layer of their own (`.plate::after`, behind the content), so a plate reads as brushed steel rather than flat paint, without any visible mottle. The grain is never a background-image on the plate itself: a background painted on the element escapes the cut corner in some engines. Every plate clips its overflow to its cut shape. Dense surfaces drop it (`.flat`: table wrappers, the log pane); Settings › Grain turns it off with the ground's grain.
- Plates (`.plate`) are the standard card, KPI tile, table wrapper, thread message, dialog, popover and toast: `panel` fill, `line` seam, the cuts above, `shadow-bevel`. An open plate (`.open`) is a container: a board column, drawn in `line-soft` with no fill, so the grain shows between its cards. A cold plate (`.cold`, dashed) is the empty state. An ember plate (`.ember`) is a thing waiting on a person: an `ember` seam and a 3px `ember` bar down its left edge. A riveted plate (`.riveted`) is a plaque bolted onto the surface behind it: square-cornered (the 2px chamfer only, never a cut, so its four rivets sit the same distance from every edge), with a slotted, domed rivet head seated in a dark countersink at each corner, centred 10px in from both edges and darker than the seam so it reads as hardware rather than a highlight. The brand plaque in the sidebar and the page header, nowhere else.
- **Text never sits on the grain.** Every heading, label, chip, control, table, footnote, loading line and empty state sits on a surface: a plate (an open plate is a container and does not count), `plain`, `solid` or `chrome`, or is itself a filled control (`btn`, `chip`, `input`). A page header is a riveted plate holding the title, its stamp line and the view's controls; a section heading is a small heading plate as wide as its text; loading lines and footnotes are small plates. A board column stays open so the grain shows between its cards, but its header row and its empty `—` are `plain`.
- Sheen marks hover: a hovered button, nav item or tab catches the light, its seam turning `gleam` and its own opaque fill taking a 10–12% gleam tint (`sheen`). A hovered card only hardens its seam to `line-strong`. Nothing glows on hover except the primary button.
- Stamp lines (`.stamp-line`: a `stamp` label between two 18px `line-strong` rules, or `.wide` to span its row) replace subtitles under page titles and name the view in the sidebar; the status bar's facts (`.facts`: `stamp` keys in `dim` before `mono` values, separated by `·`) are the same style laid in a row. Callouts (`.callout`: a 14px `gleam` tick before a `label`) head every section inside a plate or dialog.
- Icons are machined, not forged: 1.5px strokes with round caps and joins at `size-icon` (18px), every line the same weight, quiet enough that the plates and type carry the forge; see `assets/Icons/README.md`. The mark is the anvil as shipped (`assets/Logos/README.md`).

## Spacing and layout

- An 8px grid with a 64px major. `space-2` is the resting gap inside a plate; `space-3` the padding of a dense card and the horizontal inset of buttons and cells; `space-4` the padding of a standard plate; columns and the sidebar are multiples of `space-16`.
- The shell is a `size-sidebar` (236px) column of opaque `chrome` with a `line-strong` right edge and `shadow-chrome` over the main column (the sidebar sits at z-10, under the drawer and dialogs), then a main column capped at `size-content-max` with `space-4` gutters on phones and `space-8` at md and up. The sidebar holds, top to bottom: the brand plaque, the view's stamp line, the repo, the *New job or command* input (full width, with the `⌘K` hint inside it), the view nav, and at its foot the Settings item. Below md the sidebar becomes a horizontal strip of nav items with a 44px (`space-11`) settings target, and the input becomes a 44px button in the strip. The status bar is one slim opaque `chrome` row fixed at the bottom of every view, `size-statusbar` (36px) tall plus the safe-area inset, with a `line-strong` top edge: full width below md, the content column only at md and up (the sidebar keeps its full height). Left to right it holds the daemon and inbox indicators, then the facts strip (`V0.15.0 · API 3 · SHEET 1 OF 6`, with the SESSIONS live dot and count), then the model and connection indicators pushed right; each indicator opens a `plate solid` popover `space-2` above the bar. The view, the toast and (md and up) the drawer clear it. The z-order, from the back: sidebar z-10, drawer backdrop z-30, status bar z-35, drawer z-40, popovers z-42, Settings and the palette z-45, toast z-50.
- The pipeline board is four open columns (Queued, Plan, Build, Ship) in a grid of 1 column below `lg`, 2 at `lg` and 4 at `xl`, so it never scrolls sideways; at `xl` each column scrolls inside itself. Done and cancelled jobs sit in a full-width Done section below the grid when the filter holds any. The table layout is a plate around `.tbl`.
- Every text field is a recess: `.input` fills darker than the surface it sits on (a 45% mix toward `bg-deep`) under its `line` seam, so a field's edge reads at once on a grained plate; the command bar's field (`.cmd-field`) is one such recess holding the glyph, the scope chip and a seamless input.
- The drawer slides in from the right at `size-drawer-max` with `shadow-drawer`; the settings dialog (`size-dialog`) and the command bar (672px) are centred solid plates with `shadow-overlay`, full-width plates below md. Settings and the drawer sit over `backdrop`; the command bar sits over the lighter `scrim` (about half as dark), so the page or drawer it was opened from stays readable.

## Radii, borders and shadows

- Cut, never rounded: `radius-cut` (10px) and `radius-nick` (5px) are straight chisel cuts (`corner-shape: bevel`) at two opposite corners of every plate and control; `radius-1` (2px) chamfers the other two; `radius-0` on the chrome, the drawer and dialogs. Dots are circles (`radius-dot`).
- Every border is 1px. Hover never thickens a seam; it lights it (`line` → `gleam`) or hardens it (`line` → `line-strong`).
- Shadows are listed above: the bevel inside plates, the forged lip under a primary button (`shadow-primary`, a solid 2px `gleam-deep`), the overlay, drawer and chrome shadows, and the gleam's glow. A card never casts a shadow.

## States

- Hover: light the face of buttons, nav items and tabs (`gleam` seam, `sheen` tint) over 120 ms; `muted` text inside takes the control's `fg`. A card's seam hardens to `line-strong` at once while its fill stays `panel`. Table rows wash with `raised`, also at once. Sheen only turns on where the pointer can hover, so a tap on a touch screen leaves nothing behind, and it is never inherited: a chip inside a hovered control stays plain. Selected segments, primary, pressed and disabled buttons, the active nav item and the active tab never take the sheen tint. A primary button goes `gleam-hot` and glows.
- Focus: a solid 2px `line-strong` outline at 2px, on every control; checkboxes and radios too. Never a glow.
- Pressed or selected: solid `gleam` with `on-gleam` text (segments, a selected toggle, a checked box); a primary button drops onto its lip (translate 2px, no shadow); a secondary button sinks 1px into `raised`. The active nav item takes `raised`, a `line` seam, the bevel and the 3px `gleam` rule; the active tab draws a 2px `gleam` baseline and goes `fg`.
- Disabled: 40% opacity, a dashed seam, no bevel; a disabled primary button takes its surface's fill and loses its lip.
- Live: the `live` chip (gleam text on a 14% gleam tint of its surface, gleam seam), a `gleam` dot before the agent's role, and the live dot for an active daemon session: one looping `animate-ping` halo in `gleam-hot` on the live dot marks an active session; it is the only looping motion and it is off under reduced motion. Idle and waiting sessions are a hollow dot; a session waiting on a permission is the `warn` chip *needs permission*. Needs you: `ember`, the one signal for a person. Changed: `spark` (a card's `dot spark` for 3 s after a poll changes it, a drawer row that arrived since it opened, the Thread tab's `chip spark` unread count).

## Motion

Motion is limited to 120 ms colour, shadow and transform transitions on controls, the drawer's slide, and the live dot's halo: the only looping motion, off under reduced motion. Cards and table rows change at once on hover. The grain never moves. Reduced motion turns off every transition and animation: sheen appears and disappears at once, and the drawer doesn't slide or show the halo.

## Iconography and imagery

Twenty-three machined icons as described above and in `assets/Icons/README.md`; the anvil in `assets/Logos/`. Nothing else is illustrated: no photographs, no gradients, no decorative shapes beyond the grain, the cuts, the rivets and the bevel.

## Not synced

The dashboard implements Smithy: `dashboard/style.css` carries `tokens.css` verbatim and ports `smithy.css` rule for rule into its `@utility` classes (`MIGRATION.md` records the port and the few places the dashboard deliberately differs). The previews in `components/` are hand-written static renditions of the 27 families the dashboard defines, not a built bundle, so they are a reference to compare against rather than a mirror of the Svelte markup; they load the three families from Google Fonts, while the dashboard self-hosts them under `dashboard/fonts/`. The icon set in `assets/Icons/` is the one `dashboard/src/lib/icons.ts` mirrors file for file; the anvil raster in `assets/Logos/` is byte-identical to `assets/forge.png`.
