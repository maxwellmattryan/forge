A chip is a word struck on a tinted slug: the `label`-size mono word in its own colour, on a 10% tint of the surface it sits on (`panel` in plates and overlays, `chrome` on the anvil, `bg` on the ground and in board columns) inside a 45% seam of the same colour, cut `radius-nick` at the top-left and bottom-right corners like a button. Tones: `ok`, `bad`, `warn`, `info`, `gleam`, `muted` (dashed seam, no tint), `live` (gleam text on a 14% gleam tint with a full gleam seam: an agent is working), `you` (`ember` text and seam on a 12% ember tint: a person is needed, the one warm chip) and `spark` (`on-spark` text on a solid `spark` fill: unsaved or new).

Use a chip for a job's state (`StateChip`), a reviewer's verdict (`VerdictChip`, the lens name tinted by PASS/CHANGES/pending), a PR's state (`PrChip`), a stage, a round, a non-feature kind, a request status in the thread, a dependency, or a session pill. One or two words, uppercase, never wrapping, never with an icon. A chip that toggles something (the inbox log) is a `<button class="chip">`: its seam goes solid on hover and it takes a 2px outline in its own colour while expanded. Heat is never inherited, so a chip inside a hovered control stays plain.

Every tone is the same cut rectangle with the same padding; `you` and `live` differ only in colour and seam strength. Tone meanings are fixed: `you` is the only chip that means "you"; `live` the only one that means "working now"; `spark` the only solid fill. Every chip clears 4.5:1 on `bg`, `panel` and `chrome` in both themes (weakest: `gleam` on `bg` in Ash, 4.57:1).
